# Father & Son Men’s Formation Group
## Working Concept & Event Framework

> **Working vision:** A group of dads intentionally walking alongside their sons as they learn to become men who can **work, provide, protect, lead, and serve**—using practical skills, outdoor adventure, meaningful responsibility, and biblical formation—while building meaningful friendships, a sense of belonging, and relationships we hope will last a lifetime.

**Status:** Early brainstorming / working draft  
**Last updated:** October 1, 2026

---

<!-- page:vision -->
# 1. Vision

The inspiration for this group comes in part from the model of Trail Life: regular father/son gatherings, outdoor adventure, practical skills, character formation, leadership, and biblical values.

This would not simply be a collection of fun activities or a skills club. The deeper purpose would be **intentional formation**: giving boys opportunities to practice responsibility and allowing dads to walk alongside them as they grow.

A central idea:

> **We aren't just teaching boys how to do things. We're helping them become men who use their strength, skills, and character for the good of others.**

Friendship and belonging are central to the vision. We want sons to be known, welcomed, and encouraged by other boys and trusted dads. Shared work, biblical conversation, outdoor adventures, and ordinary time together can create the trust that helps friendships deepen over years. Lifelong relationships are a hope we cultivate through consistency, kindness, and showing up for one another.

The group should emphasize:

- Hard work and craftsmanship
- Responsibility and perseverance
- Providing for others
- Protecting others
- Leadership
- Servanthood
- Courage
- Stewardship
- Biblical character
- Father/son relationships
- Meaningful friendships among sons
- Connection and mutual encouragement among dads
- Belonging, hospitality, and making room for new families
- Practical competence
- Outdoor adventure

# 2. Core Rhythm

The basic rhythm should be:

**Connect → Learn → Practice → Serve → Adventure → Reflect**

Smaller gatherings introduce and practice skills and biblical principles. Those gatherings should intentionally build toward a larger outdoor or practical event where the boys have an opportunity to use what they learned.

### Smaller gatherings

Potential format:

1. **Connection** — 5–10 min: learn names, check in, and welcome anyone new
2. **Biblical principle** — 5–10 min
3. **Hands-on skill** — 30–45 min
4. **Challenge/application** — 10 min
5. **Reflection** — 5 min: share a lesson and name someone who encouraged you

Make room for a shared snack and unhurried conversation when time allows. Meet a couple of times per month, with a larger event roughly every 1–2 months; keep this rhythm flexible for families.

### Larger events

The larger event should feel like an application of the previous smaller gatherings rather than simply another activity.

The dads should provide a safe framework, but whenever practical:

> **Let the boys do the work.**

The goal is responsibility, problem-solving, perseverance, teamwork, growth, and deeper connection. Pair boys thoughtfully, rotate teams so friendships expand, and include time for play and conversation. A boy should leave knowing someone better as well as knowing how to do something new.

### Friendship in practice

- Welcome each boy by name and help newcomers join a team.
- Teach listening, encouragement, honest apologies, forgiveness, and repairing disagreements.
- Celebrate helping a friend as much as completing a task.
- Offer roles that fit different abilities; nobody has to prove he belongs.
- Let dads model friendship by checking on one another and asking for help.

Potential passages: Ecclesiastes 4:9–10; Proverbs 17:17; John 13:34–35; Romans 12:10. Read them in context and discuss one concrete way to practice them this week.

# Kickoff Big Event — "Walk Together"
## Ape Caves / Friendship / Preparation / Encouragement

**Status:** Proposed first big event for this year; date, attendance, and route are still to be decided.

### Big Event

A father/son day trip to Ape Cave near Mount St. Helens, Washington, followed by a shared meal or picnic outside the cave. Start the year with a memorable adventure that helps boys learn names, build trust, and begin friendships.

The lower cave is the proposed starting route for a mixed-experience group. Dads should decide the route after considering ages, mobility, comfort underground, current conditions, and the group's pace.

### Central question

> **"How can I help someone else take the next step, and let them help me?"**

---

## Smaller Gathering #1 — Get Connected & Get Prepared

### Skills

- Introduce yourself, learn names, and ask questions that help you know another boy.
- Pack a day bag and explain how preparation cares for your companions.
- Check a headlamp, backup lights, batteries, warm layers, and sturdy footwear.
- Practice a buddy check and clear communication on a short outdoor walk.
- Learn how a lava tube forms: flowing lava can leave a passage after a hardened outer shell forms and lava drains away.

### Hands-on practice

In pairs, review a packing list, test lights, and find out one interest and one thing each boy hopes to learn. Share your partner's answers with the group. Dads demonstrate asking for help and receiving it well.

### Biblical lesson

**We are made to walk together.**

Read Ecclesiastes 4:9–10 and Romans 12:10. Discuss what it looks like to help a friend who struggles and to welcome a boy who does not yet know anyone. Preparation is a practical expression of care.

### Connection challenge

Each boy learns two new names and gives one specific word of encouragement before leaving.

---

## Smaller Gathering #2 — Light, Trust & Helping One Another

### Skills

- Move carefully over uneven ground and communicate obstacles to a buddy.
- Use a calm voice, ask before helping, and explain when you need a pause.
- Practice stopping together if a light fails; tell a dad and switch to a working backup.
- Recognize the difference between courage and pressure to take an unnecessary risk.
- Review cave stewardship and the agreed plan for staying together.

### Hands-on practice

Create a simple ground-level obstacle course outdoors or in a familiar room with adequate lighting. Have partners give clear directions and encouragement. Practice a pretend equipment failure while keeping everyone able to see; use this to rehearse stopping and asking an adult for help.

### Biblical lesson

**God guides us; we encourage one another.**

Read Psalm 119:105 as an illustration of God's Word guiding our next steps. Read John 13:34–35 about Jesus' command to love one another and 1 Thessalonians 5:11 about encouragement. A headlamp can illustrate guidance, but Scripture's meaning is larger than the equipment analogy.

Discuss: "What helps when you feel uncertain? How can you encourage someone without making fun of their fear?"

### Connection challenge

Partners practice saying both "Can you help me?" and "How can I help?" Asking for help belongs alongside offering it.

---

## Big Event — Ape Caves Kickoff

### Suggested flow

1. **Meet and connect:** introductions, buddy pairs, and a short prayer before entry. Pair families so boys can get to know someone beyond their own household.
2. **Prepare together:** dads check gear, count the group, explain the chosen route and turnaround plan, and identify adult leaders and the adult at the back.
3. **Explore together:** boys practice noticing terrain, communicating, and encouraging their buddies. Dads retain responsibility for navigation and safety. Stop for observations only where the group can stand safely without blocking others.
4. **Gather outside:** count everyone, share a picnic or meal, and give boys time to talk and play. Keep food outside the cave.
5. **Reflect and look ahead:** name a moment of kindness, thank a buddy, and share what each boy is looking forward to next time.

### Practical lessons

- Preparation and equipment checks are ways to care for others.
- Good leadership sets a pace that keeps the group together.
- Different boys bring different strengths; everyone can contribute.
- Stewardship means respecting a place and the people sharing it.

### Biblical wrap-up

**Friendship grows when we show up, help, listen, and receive help.**

Return to Ecclesiastes 4:9–10 and John 13:34–35. Invite dads to describe a friendship that has strengthened their faith or helped them through difficulty. Connect the day's acts of care to Christ's example of love and service.

### Reflection

- What did you discover about another boy today?
- When did someone help or encourage you?
- How did you help someone else?
- What is one way you can keep building that friendship before the next gathering?

Dads offer a specific observation of each son's kindness, courage, or willingness to receive help. Avoid ranking boys by speed or how far they went.

## Planning & Preparation

### Route and access

Recreation.gov describes the lower cave as about **¾ mile one way**, with a return along the same passage; many visitors take about an hour. The upper cave is a significantly more demanding route involving rock piles, an approximately **8-foot rock wall**, and a surface return hike. It should be a separate decision based on the group's readiness, rather than an automatic extension of the kickoff.

Timed-entry reservations are required per vehicle, along with an accepted parking pass or day pass. Confirm the selected date's opening status, available reservation times, road conditions, current fees, and group requirements before committing. Some published booking text references a prior season; do not use it as confirmation of this year's dates. For questions about an organized youth group, check with the site operator.

### Family packing list

- Headlamp and two independent backup lights per person, with working batteries; this is our proposed group standard. Phone flashlights are not a substitute for proper cave lights.
- Warm layers, sturdy shoes with traction, and a small backpack.
- Drinking water and lunch for outside the cave; drinking water is not available at the site.
- Any personal essentials, with relevant needs shared privately with the supervising dads.

### Dads' responsibilities

- Confirm attendance, adult supervision, route, meeting location, transport, and a realistic turnaround time.
- Keep buddies and adult supervision together; nobody explores alone.
- Carry first-aid supplies and have an emergency plan that does not depend on cellular service underground.
- Follow posted cave rules and current cleaning/decontamination guidance for footwear and gear; avoid touching cave walls or formations.
- Do not turn off everyone's lights underground for a devotional or challenge.
- Have an alternative outdoor gathering if access closes, conditions change, or a family needs a different way to participate.

### Official planning sources

Planning notes checked October 1, 2026. Recheck before the trip:

- [Recreation.gov: Ape Cave site information and reservations](https://www.recreation.gov/timed-entry/10086990)
- [US Forest Service: Ape Cave Interpretive Site](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/giffordpinchot/recreation/ape-cave-interpretive-site)

### Decisions still open

- Date and reservation window
- Ages, number of families, and supervision arrangements
- Final route and accessibility needs
- Transport, parking passes, and shared costs
- Meal plan and weather backup

# 9. The "Dad's Challenge"

The boys aren't the only ones being challenged.

The dads should commit to **modeling the same qualities they are asking their sons to develop**.

If we teach:

- **Work** → the boys see Dad work.
- **Provision** → Dad provides.
- **Protection** → Dad protects.
- **Leadership** → Dad leads.
- **Service** → Dad serves.
- **Friendship** → Dad listens, makes time, welcomes others, and asks for help.
- **Humility** → Dad admits when he is wrong.
- **Perseverance** → Dad doesn't quit when something is difficult.

A central idea:

> **"Son, I'm still learning how to become the man God has called me to be. Come walk with me."**

This prevents the program from becoming:

> "Dad teaches boy how to be a man."

And makes it:

> **"Fathers and sons are growing together, with fathers taking responsibility for leading the way."**

# 12. Possible Long-Term Progression

One potential goal is to gradually move boys from **being taught** to **being responsible for teaching and leading**.

### Stage 1 — Learn

Dad teaches the boy.

### Stage 2 — Practice

Boy practices with Dad.

### Stage 3 — Do

Boy performs the skill with minimal help.

### Stage 4 — Lead

Boy leads another boy through the skill.

### Stage 5 — Serve

Boy uses the skill for someone else's benefit.

### Stage 6 — Teach

Boy teaches the next generation.

This creates a natural progression:

> **Learn → Do → Lead → Serve → Teach**

# 13. The Bigger Idea

The group is ultimately not about camping, carpentry, hunting, mechanics, or any individual skill.

Those are the **vehicles**.

The deeper goal is to give boys real opportunities to practice becoming men who:

The relationships formed along the way are part of that purpose. We hope sons grow into men who keep showing up for their friends, speak truth with kindness, and walk together through life. Dads can build the same kind of connection.

> **Work hard.  
> Provide faithfully.  
> Protect courageously.  
> Lead humbly.  
> Serve sacrificially.**

And to do it in the presence of fathers who are willing to say:

> **"Come with me. Let's learn this together."**

## Adventures with a purpose

These are draft event ideas, with Ape Caves being considered as our first kickoff. Each has smaller gatherings that connect the practical skills, biblical lessons, and friendships to the big experience.

### Ape Caves — Walk Together

**Proposed kickoff · Date TBD**

Explore a lava tube near Mount St. Helens, practice preparation and encouragement, and start the year by getting to know one another.

[Explore the kickoff](events/ape-caves.html)

### Be Prepared

**Camping · Responsibility**

Learn fire, shelter, and navigation, then put those skills to work on a father/son campout where everyone contributes.

[Explore the campout](events/camping.html)

### Build Something That Matters

**Carpentry · Service**

Practice tools and planning, then work together to build something useful for someone who needs it.

[Explore the service build](events/service-build.html)

### Provider’s Camp

**Fishing or hunting · Stewardship**

Learn where food comes from, prepare a shared meal, and discover how providing becomes an act of love.

[Explore the provider’s camp](events/providers-camp.html)

### Lead From the Front

**Team expedition · Servant leadership**

Bring skills together in a shared challenge, with boys learning to lead by listening, encouraging, and caring for their team.

[Explore the expedition](events/expedition.html)
