# Father & Son Men’s Formation Group
## Working Concept & Event Framework

> **Working vision:** A group of dads intentionally walking alongside their sons as they learn to become men who can **work, provide, protect, lead, and serve**—using practical skills, outdoor adventure, meaningful responsibility, and biblical formation.

**Status:** Early brainstorming / working draft  
**Last updated:** September 24, 2026

---

# 1. Vision

The inspiration for this group comes in part from the model of Trail Life: regular father/son gatherings, outdoor adventure, practical skills, character formation, leadership, and biblical values.

This would not simply be a collection of fun activities or a skills club. The deeper purpose would be **intentional formation**: giving boys opportunities to practice responsibility and allowing dads to walk alongside them as they grow.

A central idea:

> **We aren't just teaching boys how to do things. We're helping them become men who use their strength, skills, and character for the good of others.**

The group should emphasize:

- Hard work and craftsmanship
- Responsibility and perseverance
- Providing for others
- Protecting others
- Leadership
- Servanthood
- Courage
- Stewardship
- Biblical character
- Father/son relationships
- Practical competence
- Outdoor adventure

---

# 2. Core Rhythm

The basic rhythm should be:

**Learn → Practice → Serve → Adventure → Reflect**

Smaller gatherings introduce and practice skills and biblical principles. Those gatherings should intentionally build toward a larger outdoor or practical event where the boys have an opportunity to use what they learned.

### Smaller gatherings

Potential format:

1. **Dad/Son connection** — 5–10 min
2. **Biblical principle** — 5–10 min
3. **Hands-on skill** — 30–45 min
4. **Challenge/application** — 10 min
5. **Reflection** — 5 min

### Larger events

The larger event should feel like an application of the previous smaller gatherings rather than simply another activity.

The dads should provide a safe framework, but whenever practical:

> **Let the boys do the work.**

The goal is not perfection. It is responsibility, problem-solving, perseverance, teamwork, and growth.

# 4. Big Event #1 — "Be Prepared"
## Wilderness / Camping Expedition

### Big Event

A 1–2 night father/son wilderness campout.

The boys should take increasing responsibility for:

- Setting up camp
- Fire
- Cooking
- Water
- Navigation
- Camp organization
- Problem-solving
- Breaking camp
- Helping one another

### Central question

> **"Can you be counted on when things don't go according to plan?"**

---

## Smaller Gathering #1 — Fire & Shelter

### Skills

- Fire starting using multiple methods
- Fire safety
- Tarp/tent setup
- Knots
- Splitting/chopping wood
- Camp safety

### Biblical lesson

**Responsibility**

Difference between:

> "Someone will take care of it."

and:

> "I'll take care of it."

Potential passages:

- Colossians 3:23
- Proverbs 22:29
- Luke 16:10

---

## Smaller Gathering #2 — Navigation & Preparedness

### Skills

- Map and compass
- Reading terrain
- Planning a route
- Packing a day pack
- Water purification
- Basic first aid
- What to do if lost

### Biblical lesson

**Wisdom and preparation**

Preparation is one way we love and protect others.

Potential passages:

- Proverbs 27:12
- Proverbs 24:27
- Ephesians 5:15–16

---

## Big Event — Wilderness Expedition

Give the boys:

- A map
- A destination
- Basic equipment
- Responsibilities

Let them navigate, establish camp, prepare food, maintain the fire, solve problems, and help one another.

Dads maintain responsibility for safety but intentionally step back when appropriate.

### Campfire reflection

> **"A man is someone others can depend on."**

Connect this to Christ: Jesus used His strength, authority, and abilities for the good of others.

- Garden beds

---

## Smaller Gathering #1 — Tools & Craftsmanship

### Skills

- Measuring
- Squaring
- Reading plans
- Hammer/nail/screw
- Drill
- Saw
- Tool safety
- Material estimation

Possible mini-project:

**Build a simple wooden toolbox.**

**Work has dignity.**

---

## Smaller Gathering #2 — Planning & Leadership

Introduce the actual service project.

Have the boys:

- Determine materials
- Make a shopping list
- Estimate quantities
- Estimate cost
- Create a construction plan
- Divide responsibilities
- Identify potential problems

### Biblical lesson

**Leadership means responsibility, not authority.**

Potential passage:

- Luke 14:28 — count the cost before building.

A leader should think about:

- What will this require?
- Who needs to be involved?
- What could go wrong?
- How can we help one another succeed?

---

## Big Event — Service Build

Give the boys:

- Materials
- Plans
- Tools
- Safety framework

Then let them build.

Dads supervise safety and teach as needed, but boys should do as much of the actual work as reasonably possible.

Have the recipient see the finished project.

### Final lesson

> **"Strength is given to us for a purpose."**

Christlike strength isn't primarily about what we can get for ourselves; it is about what we can do for others.

# 6. Big Event #3 — "Provide"
## Hunting / Fishing / Food

### Big Event

A father/son fishing or hunting camp where the boys participate in harvesting food and then prepare a meal.

The emphasis is not simply:

> "Can you catch or kill something?"

Instead:

> **"Can you understand where food comes from, responsibly steward God's creation, and provide for others?"**

---

## Smaller Gathering #1 — Understanding Provision

### Fishing options

- Rod/reel setup
- Knots
- Casting
- Bait/lures
- Reading water
- Fish identification
- Cleaning fish

### Hunting options

- Wildlife identification
- Tracking
- Habitat
- Conservation
- Ethics
- Firearm/bow safety
- Field dressing

For younger boys, hunting can begin with wildlife, tracking, conservation, and safety before progressing to harvesting.

### Biblical lesson

**A provider thinks beyond himself.**

Food is easy to take for granted. Someone had to:

- Grow it
- Raise it
- Hunt it
- Catch it
- Harvest it
- Process it
- Preserve it

Ultimately:

> **God provides. We are stewards of what He provides.**

## Smaller Gathering #2 — From Harvest to Table

- Butchering/processing
- Fish cleaning
- Meat preservation
- Smoking
- Canning
- Cooking over fire
- Food storage
- Basic nutrition

### Biblical lesson

**Providing is an act of love.**

It can include:

- Food
- Shelter
- Security
- Wisdom
- Encouragement

Before the event, the boys plan:

- Ingredients

Connect provision to Christ's example of giving Himself for others.

The key question becomes:

> "If you're the leader, who are you responsible for?"

> "Who has to listen to you?"

> **"Who are you responsible to serve?"**

- Mark 10:43–45

## Smaller Gathering #2 — Protection

### Skills

- First aid
- CPR basics
- Emergency response
- Fire safety
- Emergency shelter
- Situational awareness
- Helping an injured person
- Calling for help
- Wilderness emergencies

Practice scenarios:

> "Your buddy falls and can't walk."

> "Someone gets lost."

> "There is a fire."

> "Someone is injured and scared."

### Biblical lesson

**Protection isn't domination.**

A protector uses strength and courage for the good of others while also using wisdom rather than recklessness.

Potential passages:

- Psalm 82:3–4

- Proverbs 27:12

---

## Big Event — The Expedition

Give the boys a mission such as:

> "Get from Point A to Point B, establish a temporary camp, complete several challenges, prepare lunch, and bring everyone safely back."

Possible challenges:

- Navigate to a location
- Build a shelter
- Start a fire
- Complete a first-aid scenario
- Move a team member safely
- Solve a construction/problem-solving challenge
- Prepare food

Dads follow as much as practical while retaining ultimate responsibility for safety.

---

## Final Reflection

Ask each boy:

1. **What did you do well?**
2. **Where did you fail?**
3. **What will you do differently next time?**

Then dads speak specifically into their sons' growth.

Instead of simply:

> "Good job."

Try:

> "I watched you get frustrated when the map didn't make sense. You wanted to quit, but you stopped, thought about it, and figured it out. That's something I want you to remember."

# 9. The "Dad's Challenge"

The boys aren't the only ones being challenged.

The dads should commit to **modeling the same qualities they are asking their sons to develop**.

If we teach:

- **Work** → the boys see Dad work.
- **Provision** → Dad provides.
- **Protection** → Dad protects.
- **Leadership** → Dad leads.
- **Service** → Dad serves.
- **Humility** → Dad admits when he is wrong.
- **Perseverance** → Dad doesn't quit when something is difficult.

A central idea:

> **"Son, I'm still learning how to become the man God has called me to be. Come walk with me."**

This prevents the program from becoming:

> "Dad teaches boy how to be a man."

And makes it:

> **"Fathers and sons are growing together, with fathers taking responsibility for leading the way."**

# 10. Potential Program Identity

## Draft Name: The Forge

**The Forge** is the current favorite working name, but it is intentionally a **draft name** while the group takes shape.

The metaphor fits the concept well: a forge is a place where something is shaped through **heat, pressure, work, and time**.

That connects naturally with the group's core themes:

- **Work** — learning to do hard things
- **Provide** — learning to build and care for others
- **Protect** — developing strength and courage
- **Lead** — learning to take responsibility
- **Serve** — using strength and skill for the good of others

A possible theme verse to explore:

> **"Iron sharpens iron, and one man sharpens another." — Proverbs 27:17**

The name should not imply that dads have already "arrived" as men and are simply manufacturing their sons into men. A better picture is:

> **Fathers and sons are being shaped together, with fathers taking responsibility for leading the way.**

# 12. Possible Long-Term Progression

One potential goal is to gradually move boys from **being taught** to **being responsible for teaching and leading**.

### Stage 1 — Learn

Dad teaches the boy.

### Stage 2 — Practice

Boy practices with Dad.

### Stage 3 — Do

Boy performs the skill with minimal help.

### Stage 4 — Lead

Boy leads another boy through the skill.

### Stage 5 — Serve

Boy uses the skill for someone else's benefit.

### Stage 6 — Teach

Boy teaches the next generation.

This creates a natural progression:

> **Learn → Do → Lead → Serve → Teach**

# 13. The Bigger Idea

The group is ultimately not about camping, carpentry, hunting, mechanics, or any individual skill.

Those are the **vehicles**.

The deeper goal is to give boys real opportunities to practice becoming men who:

> **Work hard.  
> Provide faithfully.  
> Protect courageously.  
> Lead humbly.  
> Serve sacrificially.**

And to do it in the presence of fathers who are willing to say:

> **"Come with me. Let's learn this together."**
